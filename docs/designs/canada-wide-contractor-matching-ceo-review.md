## Vision

Tiling.ca becomes a Canada-ready, tile-specific contractor-referral service without pretending to provide installation work or coverage it has not earned. Ontario is the validation market. Province and city pages become public and indexable only after real contractor coverage, content quality, operations, and privacy gates pass.

### 10x Check

The ambitious version is not a larger directory. It is a trustworthy matching network where every public location corresponds to usable supply, every homeowner request receives an explicit outcome, and every expansion decision is driven by observed demand plus reviewed contractor capacity. Over time, the same registry, intake, audit trail, and coverage gates can support additional provinces without creating doorway pages or weakening the homeowner promise.

## Contractor Application Contract

Release one accepts applications only from contractors seeking Ontario coverage. The page may explain the Canada-wide direction, but a valid out-of-province selection receives a clear “Ontario pilot only” business outcome and is not persisted. A future-market contractor waitlist is not part of release one.

No files are uploaded in release one. Insurance and work evidence are declarations plus HTTPS URLs. The operator must not automatically fetch submitted URLs.

## Contractor Application Lifecycle

```text
SUBMITTED
├── duplicate confirmed --------------------------> DUPLICATE
├── applicant withdraws --------------------------> WITHDRAWN
├── operator requests missing evidence ----------> NEEDS_INFORMATION
│   ├── applicant responds ----------------------> UNDER_REVIEW
│   └── 30 days without response ----------------> EXPIRED
└── complete application ------------------------> UNDER_REVIEW
    ├── policy checks fail -----------------------> REJECTED
    ├── operator needs clarification -------------> NEEDS_INFORMATION
    └── operator approves ------------------------> APPROVED_PENDING_PROMOTION
                                                   ├── atomic promotion succeeds -> PROMOTED
                                                   │    ├── Contractor
                                                   │    └── ContractorCoverage
                                                   └── promotion fails -> remain pending
                                                        └── admin retries or rejects
```

| Transition | Actor | Preconditions | Applicant outcome / audit rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| New → Submitted | System | Valid input and durable CRM write | Show reference; send receipt; audit consent version |
| Submitted → Duplicate | Operator | Duplicate candidate reviewed and confirmed | Explain that an existing application is being used; no second promotion |
| Any unpromoted state → Withdrawn | Applicant or operator | Authenticated email/reply process confirms withdrawal | Stop review and promotion work; keep all coverage inactive; apply deletion/retention policy; audit without retained deleted content |
| Submitted → Needs information | Operator | Required evidence is missing or ambiguous | Send a specific request and 30-day deadline |
| Submitted/Needs information → Under review | Operator | Minimum review evidence present | Tell applicant review is manual and no coverage claim is active |
| Under review → Needs information | Operator | Review uncovers missing or conflicting evidence | Send a specific request and 30-day deadline; return to Under review only after operator confirms the response |
| Needs information → Expired | System or operator | No response for 30 days | Notify applicant; terminal unless a new application is submitted |
| Under review → Rejected | Operator | Documented policy reason | Send non-defamatory outcome and correction channel; audit reason in restricted view |
| Under review → Approved pending promotion | Administrator | Review policy passes | Approval is not public coverage; begin atomic promotion |
| Approved pending promotion → Promoted | Administrator/system | Current participation terms are accepted and version/time recorded; idempotent transaction commits valid Contractor and ContractorCoverage records, then activates the coverage snapshot | Notify operator; only active records can affect computed coverage or receive invitations |
| Approved pending promotion → Rejected | Administrator | Promotion cannot be safely completed or later evidence invalidates approval | No active coverage remains; applicant receives a documented outcome |

Application records never contribute to `computedCoverageStatus`. Promotion is an administrator-only, idempotent transaction that creates or updates the stable `Contractor` record and explicit `ContractorCoverage` records, records the source application and review snapshot, and activates coverage only after every required policy and expiry field passes. On partial failure, no coverage record becomes active, the application remains `APPROVED_PENDING_PROMOTION`, and the operator receives a visible retryable error. Re-running promotion must not duplicate contractors or coverage. `PROMOTED` requires acceptance of the current participation terms with version and timestamp. Draft or inactive coverage created during a failed or incomplete promotion is excluded from matching, city eligibility, and indexing calculations.

Release-one corrections are operator-assisted. The applicant replies through the address/thread associated with the receipt or contacts the published channel and confirms control of the original email or phone. Corrections are allowed in `SUBMITTED`, `NEEDS_INFORMATION`, and `UNDER_REVIEW`; every changed field produces an audit event. A change to geography, categories, insurance, work evidence, identity, or availability returns the application to `UNDER_REVIEW` and invalidates any prior review decision. After promotion, corrections follow the contractor-review workflow rather than editing the original application.

Duplicate candidates are detected from normalized business name plus email or phone, exact email or phone reuse, or overlapping business identity and Ontario coverage submitted within 30 days. Detection never automatically merges records. An operator may mark the records distinct, link the new submission as evidence on the existing application, or supersede the older unpromoted application; each choice requires a reason and audit event. A promoted contractor is updated only through the promotion/review workflow, never by directly merging public-form input.

## Applicant Privacy and Public Outcomes

- Publish the application purpose, review process, absence of guaranteed leads, compensation disclosure, data uses, retention, correction, withdrawal, and deletion channels before collecting production applications.
- Version application-processing consent separately from any future marketing system. Never subscribe an applicant to marketing by default.
- Restrict insurance details and work-example links to the operator/administrator roles. Do not expose applications publicly or include applicant personal data in analytics.
- Delete rejected, withdrawn, expired, and never-promoted applications after 90 days unless legal review establishes a different justified period. Retain promoted application evidence according to the contractor review and audit policy.
- A CRM/persistence failure shows no receipt and asks the applicant to retry or contact the operator. A successful durable write shows a stable reference even when email delivery later fails.
- Duplicate detection shows a neutral receipt and routes the record for operator confirmation; it must not reveal another person’s or company’s stored details.
- Delayed review messaging must state the current Ontario pilot and avoid promising a response time until the queue owner has measured capacity.

## Contractor Intake Acceptance Criteria

- Client and server reject missing, malformed, over-limit, unsafe URL, and inconsistent controlled values with accessible field errors. A valid out-of-Ontario selection produces a non-persistent business outcome rather than a validation error.
- Double submission and retry with the same idempotency key create one application; possible cross-key duplicates enter review without exposing stored applicant data.
- Honeypot and throttling suppress abuse without silently storing a real applicant as spam.
- A receipt appears only after durable CRM persistence; confirmation-email failure is retried and surfaced to operations without rolling back the application.
- Every lifecycle transition records actor, reason, timestamp, previous/new state, consent version, and correlation ID.
- Withdrawal, correction, deletion, approval, rejection, expiry, duplicate resolution, and promotion failure are tested.
- A happy-path integration test proves that one approved application idempotently creates or updates one stable contractor, creates the intended coverage records once, activates them only after the transaction succeeds, and then recomputes coverage eligibility.
- An unreviewed, rejected, withdrawn, expired, or partially promoted application can never affect matching or city/province indexing.
- Keyboard navigation, labels, error summaries, focus movement, touch targets, contrast, loading, duplicate-click, slow-network, and success states meet the release-one WCAG 2.2 AA target.

## Architecture Decisions

### Operational and public coverage boundary

The managed CRM remains authoritative for live matching eligibility. Qualification and pre-introduction checks query the CRM and fail closed when fresh eligibility cannot be confirmed. Public pages, navigation, canonicals, and sitemaps read a versioned, last-known-good coverage publication snapshot rather than querying the CRM during page requests.

CRM changes pass through an authenticated, idempotent synchronization service that validates the complete candidate snapshot before atomic publication. Each snapshot records schema version, source revision, generation time, content-review revision, active locations/categories, and a checksum. Invalid or partial snapshots are rejected without replacing the last-known-good version. Coverage loss blocks matching within five minutes and removes affected locations from sitemap/navigation within the approved 60-minute target; alerting covers missed or rejected synchronization.

### Environment and storage adapter boundary

Forms depend on an explicit `SubmissionRepository` boundary with separate development and production implementations. Development may write scrubbed test submissions to local JSONL or a local D1 database and must display a persistent preview banner. Production requires the configured CRM adapter and validated secrets at startup/deploy health check. Missing or invalid production configuration disables submission endpoints with a named configuration error and user-visible unavailable state; it never falls back to local files or a secondary D1 source of truth.

Only the production CRM adapter may emit a production receipt. Environment checks, test fixtures, and deployment smoke tests prove that local adapters cannot be selected under the production runtime mode.

## Error and Rescue Decisions

### Ambiguous CRM persistence timeout

Every homeowner and contractor submission carries a stable idempotency key. If the CRM create/update request times out after transmission, the server retries with the same key and queries the CRM by that key before deciding the response. A confirmed record returns the one stable receipt; a confirmed absence may be retried within the bounded policy; an unresolved result returns a named retryable unavailable error and alerts operations. The site never claims success without durable evidence and never changes the key during retry.

### Duplicate and out-of-order coverage events

Every CRM coverage event carries a monotonically increasing source revision for its aggregate or stream. The sync service acknowledges an already-applied revision without rewriting, rejects and audits an older revision, and applies a newer revision only after complete validation. A detected revision gap, conflicting payload for the same revision, or inability to confirm current CRM state leaves the last-known-good public snapshot intact, blocks affected matching when freshness exceeds its limit, alerts operations, and schedules a full reconciliation rather than guessing from timestamps.

### Manual contractor invitations

Release one does not automatically send, retry, or advance contractor invitations. The founder/operator selects one eligible contractor, generates the signed single-use response link through the CRM integration, sends the invitation manually from the authenticated CRM/email workflow, and records the sent time and provider outcome. A reported delivery failure is recorded explicitly and the operator decides whether to resend or select the next eligible contractor. The system must not treat a failed delivery as a contractor decline or start an unobservable automatic sequence.

## Security Decisions

### Coverage webhook authenticity and replay protection

The synchronization endpoint verifies an HMAC signature computed over the exact raw request body plus provider timestamp and event ID before parsing or processing the payload. It rejects signatures with constant-time comparison, timestamps outside the configured short window, reused event IDs, unsupported algorithms, and unknown key IDs. Secrets live only in managed environment storage, logs never contain them, and rotation supports an explicitly bounded overlap between old and new keys. Authentication failures never change coverage state and emit security metrics with rate-based alerts.

### CRM identity and authorization boundary

Every human uses an individual CRM identity protected by MFA. Operator, administrator, supply-review, and privacy/deletion permissions are distinct and follow least privilege; shared logins are prohibited. The site integration uses a separate non-human service identity scoped only to the records and operations required for submission, eligibility checks, signed-link creation, and coverage synchronization. Human and service access is auditable, revocable independently, reviewed on a documented cadence, and removed immediately when no longer required. Production secrets are never available to client code, preview environments, source control, or ordinary operator accounts.

## Data Flow and Interaction Decisions

### Refresh-safe submission identity

The form creates its idempotency key before the first submission attempt and stores only that key and creation time in `sessionStorage`. Refresh, back/forward navigation, and retries reuse the key until the CRM confirms success or 24 hours elapse. Confirmed success clears the key before another request can start. Names, contact details, project descriptions, consent choices, and contractor evidence are not stored in browser persistence by this mechanism.

```text
FORM INPUT ──▶ CLIENT VALIDATION ──▶ SERVER VALIDATION ──▶ CRM WRITE ──▶ RECEIPT
    │                 │                     │                 │             │
    ├─ nil/empty ─────┴──────────────▶ field errors; no persistence        │
    ├─ wrong/long/unsafe ────────────▶ field errors; no persistence        │
    ├─ valid unsupported geography ──▶ honest unavailable outcome          │
    │                                  └─ homeowner waitlist consent only  │
    ├─ duplicate click/refresh ──────▶ reuse same idempotency key           │
    ├─ CRM 429/timeout/5xx ──────────▶ bounded retry/reconcile; no false receipt
    └─ durable CRM success ────────────────────────────────────────────────▶ stable reference

Published location data:
CRM event ─▶ authenticate ─▶ revision check ─▶ validate full snapshot ─▶ atomic publish
    │              │               │                   │
    ├─ missing ────┴───────────────┴───────────────────┴─▶ no state change
    ├─ old/duplicate ─────────────────────────────────────▶ acknowledge/ignore safely
    ├─ gap/error ─────────────────────────────────────────▶ retain last-known-good + reconcile
    └─ valid newer revision ──────────────────────────────▶ pages/nav/sitemap update together
```

Interaction rules: disable duplicate submit while a request is in flight; restore the button on a recoverable error; keep server-returned field errors associated with inputs; do not reset form values on failure; move focus to the error summary; show success only with the stable reference; and treat navigation away as cancellation of the browser wait, not cancellation of a potentially committed CRM operation.

## Code Quality Decisions

### Central location registry

Create one typed location registry and pure selectors as the only application-level source for province/city identity, publication lifecycle, supported categories, coverage freshness, content-review state, canonical path, indexability, navigation visibility, sitemap membership, and matching eligibility. Public selectors consume the validated publication snapshot; live matching selectors additionally require fresh CRM confirmation. Routes, metadata, structured data, breadcrumbs, redirect rules, navigation, and sitemap generation call these selectors rather than duplicating conditions.

Separate business identity, services, materials, locations, and operational state instead of extending the current monolithic `app/data.ts`. Registry validation fails the build/publication candidate for duplicate slugs, invalid transitions, unsupported categories, stale reviews, canonical collisions, missing province relationships, or an indexable record that fails any required gate.

## Test Strategy

Use a test pyramid: many pure unit tests for validators, state transitions, selectors, coverage calculations, business-hour clocks, token rules, and error mapping; integration tests for form-to-repository, CRM-to-snapshot, promotion, redirects, metadata, sitemap, and accessibility states; and a small number of browser E2E journeys. The default suite uses a deterministic in-process fake `SubmissionRepository`/CRM adapter with controllable success, nil, empty, timeout, 429, malformed payload, duplicate, partial failure, stale revision, and out-of-order event modes.

A separately triggered contract suite uses the chosen CRM/email sandbox to verify field schemas, permissions, idempotent create/update, lookup by key, webhook signature payloads, revision behavior, audit fields, deletion/export, email delivery events, and provider rate-limit/error shapes. Sandbox tests use isolated test records, never production PII, and clean up by stable run ID. CI does not make every unit test depend on an external provider; release readiness requires the latest contract suite evidence for the configured provider version.

## Performance Review

No new unresolved performance issue was found. Public rendering uses a bounded versioned snapshot and pure selectors rather than per-request CRM calls. CRM calls occur on submissions and controlled operator transitions, not on ordinary page views. The implementation must index stable IDs, idempotency keys, state/queue timestamps, contractor-coverage relationships, and source revisions in whichever provider/query layer is selected; paginate operator queues; bound snapshot size; reuse HTTP connections where supported; and measure p95/p99 submission and eligibility-check latency during the Phase 0 spike.

## Observability Decisions

Provide a day-one operational view using the chosen hosting/provider telemetry plus CRM reports; a custom analytics product is not required. Every event uses stable request/application/location IDs and a correlation ID, but logs, traces, metrics, and alerts contain no homeowner contact details or free-text project notes.

Required panels and alerts cover submission success/failure by endpoint and named error, CRM latency/429/5xx/unknown outcomes, confirmation-email failures, manual invitation delivery outcomes, snapshot source revision and age, rejected/gapped sync revisions, locations currently blocked by stale coverage, matching eligibility failures, applications/requests by lifecycle state, oldest unhandled queue item, and promotion failures. Runbooks name the founder/operator action for each alert, including how to disable forms, reconcile a submission, restore a last-known-good snapshot, pause a location, rotate a secret, and handle privacy/deletion requests.

Entry, exit, and significant state-transition logs are structured. Cross-service calls propagate the correlation ID. Alerts are thresholded to avoid single-event noise where appropriate, except security failures, production configuration errors, unknown persistence outcomes, and partial promotion, which surface immediately.

## Long-Term Trajectory Review

Reversibility is **4/5**: public routes, forms, indexing, and redirects are gated and reversible; the durable CRM data model and URL migration become path-dependent once real records and search equity accumulate. The chosen registry, repository adapters, versioned snapshots, stable IDs, audit events, and deterministic gates create a platform for later provinces, provider replacement, applicant status tools, match explanations, and coverage analytics without requiring a province-by-city-by-service page matrix.

Phase 2 may add the deferred homeowner status page, match-rationale presentation, and CRM-derived demand/coverage dashboard only after pilot evidence. Phase 3 may activate another province in small batches after Ontario meets the assignment and a language/legal/operations review passes. Contractor accounts, public profiles, automated ranking, reviews, payments, SMS, uploads, and Quebec remain separate future decisions.

## Design and UX Decisions

### Homeowner-first referral positioning

The primary action across the header, hero, location pages, service/material guides, empty/unavailable states, and footer is **“Find a tile contractor”** and leads to `/request-a-match`. **“Join as a contractor”** is a clearly visible secondary action leading to `/for-contractors`. Do not use “Get a quote,” “Book an installer,” installer phone framing, or copy implying that Tiling.ca performs, prices, warrants, or schedules installation work.

The first viewport communicates in order: tile-specific contractor matching; current Ontario availability/limits; the small set of project details needed; how contractor review and data sharing work; and the primary action. Trust content explains what Tiling.ca checks, what it does not verify, how the free contractor pilot works, why a match may be unavailable, and that the homeowner chooses whether to hire the introduced contractor.

### Guided homeowner intake

Use a mobile-first multi-step form with these logical steps: **Location → Project → Timing & budget → Contact & consent → Review**. The progress indicator names the current step without promising a misleading percentage. Back/forward controls preserve current in-memory values, focus the step heading, announce validation errors, and never submit until the final review action. Only the idempotency key persists across refresh; form PII does not.

        ├─ malformed postal code ─▶ inline error

Every screen defines loading, empty/not-applicable, validation error, provider error, success, and partial/uncertain states. Touch targets, contrast, labels, summaries, keyboard order, screen-reader announcements, reduced motion, and responsive layouts target WCAG 2.2 AA.
