# Design: Coverage-Gated Canada-Wide Tile Contractor Matching

## Problem Statement

Tiling.ca currently presents itself as a tile installation company serving five North GTA cities. The intended business is different: a Canada-wide lead-generation and contractor-referral service that helps homeowners without a trusted renovation network find a suitable local tile installer.

The national expansion must create genuine homeowner value without claiming that Tiling.ca performs installations or has active contractor coverage where it does not. It must also avoid a large set of substantially similar city pages created primarily for search rankings.

## Demand Evidence

- The project is pre-launch and has not yet completed a paid referral or a homeowner-to-installer match through tiling.ca.
- Existing demand evidence is one-sided: contractors and suppliers have expressed interest in receiving more projects.
- Homeowner demand for a tile-specific referral service remains unvalidated.

## Status Quo

Homeowners commonly rely on a general contractor, renovation professional, or tile supplier to recommend an installer. A homeowner without that network must search independently, judge qualifications, and determine whether a contractor is suitable for the material, assembly, project size, location, and schedule.

Broad Canadian marketplaces already offer trade and city directories. Tiling.ca should not compete by reproducing a smaller generic directory. It should qualify tile projects more deeply and make a narrower, higher-confidence match.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The first customer is a Canadian homeowner who has a real tile project but no trusted renovation professional to recommend an installer.

The narrowest useful product is:

1. The homeowner submits a postal code, project type, surface, material, approximate size, project stage, desired timing, notes, and contact details. File uploads are deferred until secure storage and deletion are designed.
2. Tiling.ca determines whether an active installer in that service area matches the project.
3. The homeowner receives a clear response: matched, more information required, waitlisted because coverage is unavailable, or declined with an explanation.
4. The contractor receives only projects that match declared geography, specialties, project size, and availability.

The initial active market remains Ontario, beginning with the existing North GTA coverage. The national architecture may contain planned locations, but search indexing is earned by real coverage and page usefulness.

## Constraints

- Tiling.ca is a referral service, not the installer of record.
- No city or province may imply active contractor coverage unless coverage is reviewed and current.
- No fabricated offices, addresses, ratings, project counts, credentials, or local experience claims.
- Inactive or incomplete location pages must not appear in the XML sitemap and must use `noindex` when publicly reachable.
- City content must help a homeowner make or complete a match; city-name substitutions are insufficient.
- The existing services, materials, area routes, quote flow, data-driven rendering, metadata, structured data, and sitemap generation should be reused where they fit.
- Nothing is published as part of this design phase.

## Premises

1. Tiling.ca is a contractor-referral service and does not perform installations.
2. The first customer is a homeowner without a trusted contractor network.
3. The core value is tile-specific project qualification followed by a suitable local match.
4. Contractors and suppliers provide the initial supply network, while homeowner demand still needs validation.
5. Province and city pages become indexable only when the location has real matching coverage and useful local information.
6. Success is measured by qualified requests and successful matches, not published page count.

## Approaches Considered

- **Ontario validation first:** safest and fastest, but delays meaningful Canada-wide targeting.
- **Coverage-gated national framework:** selected because it supports national growth while preventing unsupported claims and thin-page indexing.
- **Postal-code demand map only:** strong demand test, but gives up city-level organic acquisition during validation.

## Recommended Approach

Build a coverage-gated national information architecture with one source of truth for provinces, cities, coverage, content quality, and indexing eligibility.

### Release boundary

The architecture is Canada-ready, but release one is an Ontario validation pilot:

- Reposition the existing site honestly as a referral service.
- Launch the matching intake without file uploads.
- Seed Ontario and the existing five North GTA cities only.
- Publish the Canada locations hub, Ontario page, and only those city pages that pass coverage and content gates.
- Operate matching manually in an authenticated CRM or equivalent system selected before launch.
- Defer other province records, contractor profiles, payments, reviews, automated ranking, SMS, photo uploads, and city × service pages.

The system may define reusable province and city types now, but it must not seed speculative national records merely to create routes.

### Public information architecture

```text
/
├── /services
│   └── /services/[service]
├── /materials
│   └── /materials/[material]
├── /locations
│   ├── /locations/[province]
│   └── /locations/[province]/[city]
├── /how-it-works
├── /for-contractors
└── /request-a-match
```

The existing `/areas` routes should redirect to the corresponding Ontario location pages after equivalent pages exist. Redirects must preserve the current Newmarket, Aurora, Richmond Hill, North York, and Vaughan URLs' accumulated value.

Do not create a province × city × service cross-product in the first release. City pages link to the canonical service and material guides. A city-and-service page becomes eligible only if it later contains distinct local availability, proof, project data, and homeowner utility beyond the parent city page.

### Conceptual data model

```text
Province 1──* City 1──* ContractorCoverage *──1 Contractor
                   │
                   └──* ReferralRequest 1──* MatchInvitation *──1 Contractor
                                      │
                                      └──0..1 Match

Every mutable record ──* AuditEvent
```

- **Province:** slug, name, abbreviation, public content, lifecycle state, content review, index decision.
- **City:** province ID, slug, municipality, public content, `publicationLifecycleState`, content review, index decision.
- **Contractor:** stable ID, business identity, review status, review expiry, contact channel, active/suspended state.
- **ContractorCoverage:** contractor ID, city ID, included/excluded forward sortation areas, supported service categories, materials, project-size bands, availability state, last reviewed time.
- **ReferralRequest:** stable ID, normalized contact hash for duplicate detection, postal code, municipality supplied, project fields, consent version/time, lifecycle state, evaluated coverage snapshot, created/updated times.
- **MatchInvitation:** stable ID, request ID, contractor ID, invitation order, summary shared, sent/expiry/response times, accepted or declined reason.
- **Match:** request ID, accepted contractor ID, introduction time, closure reason, outcome time.
- **AuditEvent:** actor, record type/ID, previous state, new state, reason, timestamp, and request correlation ID.

City `computedCoverageStatus` is derived from active, unexpired `ContractorCoverage` records; it is not hand-entered separately. Category support belongs to the contractor-city relationship, not the city itself. Matching eligibility follows computed coverage. Public routes and indexing follow the separate publication lifecycle plus the quality gates.

### Geographic registry

Each province record includes:

- slug, name, abbreviation, and display order;
- computed coverage status and publication lifecycle state;
- supported cities;
- province-level climate, logistics, and homeowner guidance;
- content-review timestamps;
- indexing eligibility and the reasons for the current decision.

Each city record includes:

- slug, name, province, and postal-code prefixes served;
- computed coverage status, publication lifecycle state, and reviewed contractor count;
- computed supported services, materials, project sizes, and availability notes;
- local homeowner guidance and access/logistics considerations;
- unique frequently asked questions where evidence supports them;
- content quality checks, last review date, and indexing eligibility;
- optional proof such as completed matches or reviewed contractor coverage, added only when real.

### Publication lifecycle and coverage triggers

```text
PLANNED -> RECRUITING -> PUBLISHED -> PAUSED -> RETIRED
   |           |           |         |
   |           |           |         +-> noindex, removed from sitemap
   |           |           +-> indexable only after quality gate passes
   |           +-> may accept contractor applications; homeowner waitlist only
   +-> no public SEO page required

PUBLISHED -> PAUSED when computed coverage drops below the minimum
PAUSED -> PUBLISHED only after coverage and content are re-reviewed
```

Coverage and publication are separate decisions. `computedCoverageStatus=ELIGIBLE` is necessary but not sufficient for `publicationLifecycleState=PUBLISHED` or indexing.

| Transition | Actor | Preconditions and trigger | Required action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planned → Recruiting | Operator | City approved for supply recruitment | Record target categories and recruiting owner |
| Recruiting → Published | Operator | Computed coverage threshold passes | Save coverage snapshot, review content, run index gate |
| Published → Paused | System or operator | Coverage falls below threshold, review expires, or concern is raised | Stop matching, remove from sitemap, alert operator |
| Paused → Published | Operator | Concern resolved and all gates re-run | Audit decision and restore only after tests pass |
| Paused → Retired | Administrator | Location will not return or an equivalent page replaces it | Apply permanent retirement response and audit reason |

Pilot definitions:

- A **reviewed contractor** has confirmed identity and business contact, declared service areas and categories, provided work examples, and supplied current insurance information or an explicit disclosure that insurance was not independently verified. The public site must describe the checks exactly and must not use “verified” until the final policy supports that word.
- Contractor review expires after 12 months or earlier when a required document expires.
- A city has `computedCoverageStatus=ELIGIBLE` for release one when at least two reviewed contractors accept bathroom/shower projects and at least two reviewed contractors accept floor-tile projects there. Any additional category may be listed publicly only when at least two reviewed contractors accept it.
- Coverage is revalidated when a request is qualified and again before an introduction. If it changes, the saved evaluation snapshot remains auditable and the homeowner receives the new outcome.

### City indexing quality gate

A city is indexable only when every required condition passes:

1. The city has `computedCoverageStatus=ELIGIBLE`, `publicationLifecycleState=PUBLISHED`, and lists only categories meeting the two-contractor threshold.
2. Postal-code or municipal coverage boundaries are recorded.
3. The matching workflow can return an honest outcome for that location.
4. A named human reviewer attests that the page contains location-specific homeowner guidance rather than token replacement; automated tests verify the attestation is present and current.
5. Title, description, canonical URL, breadcrumbs, internal links, and structured data describe the referral service accurately.
6. The page has a current `lastReviewedAt` value and a named review reason.
7. Automated tests confirm indexable pages appear in crawlable navigation and sitemaps while inactive pages do not.

### Province indexing quality gate

A province page is indexable only when it has at least two indexable city pages, reviewed province-specific homeowner guidance, accurate language/coverage disclosures, canonical metadata, internal links, and a current content review. Release one may index Ontario after those conditions pass. Other provinces appear as plain-text “not yet available” options in the national intake and do not receive public routes.

| Province state | Public route | Sitemap/navigation |
|---|---|---|
| Zero indexable cities | 200 `noindex` only when useful Ontario pilot/waitlist information exists; otherwise 404 | Excluded |
| One indexable city | 200 `noindex` with that city linked and limits stated | Excluded |
| Two or more indexable cities and province gate passes | 200 and indexable | Included |
| Previously indexed but gate later fails | 200 with current coverage and `noindex`; may return after review | Removed immediately |

### Route behavior

| Coverage/content state | Public route | Sitemap/navigation |
|---|---|---|
| Planned or Recruiting | 404; parent may list the city as plain text with a waitlist action | Excluded |
| Published and both gates pass | 200 and indexable | Included |
| Coverage eligible but a content/index gate fails | Preview-only; public 404 | Excluded |
| Paused after previously being indexed | 200 with honest unavailable notice and `noindex`; may return to Published | Removed immediately |
| Retired | Permanent 308 only when a genuinely equivalent page exists; otherwise 410 | Excluded |

Primary navigation and crawlable location lists contain only indexable pages. Recruiting locations may be named without links beside the national waitlist intake.

### Matching intake

Replace the generic local quote form with a referral intake that captures:

- postal code and municipality;
- residential project; commercial matching is deferred from release one;
- room or surface type;
- installation, repair, regrouting, or consultation need;
- tile material and format when known;
- approximate area and budget band;
- project stage and desired start window;
- supporting notes; secure photo upload is explicitly deferred from release one;
- homeowner contact and consent to share the request with matched contractors.

Release-one validation and failure rules:

- Malformed Canadian postal codes fail inline and server-side validation. A valid but uncovered postal code first shows the unsupported outcome without persistence; only explicit waitlist consent creates an `UNSUPPORTED` request.
- Forward sortation areas are routing hints, not proof of a municipal boundary. Municipality text is normalized for display; explicit operator include/exclude overrides determine coverage when the two disagree. A licensed postal dataset is required before any finer-grained automatic mapping.
- A client-generated idempotency key prevents repeat writes. The same normalized contact, postal code, and category within 24 hours is flagged for operator review instead of creating another contractor invitation.
- Honeypot, request throttling, field limits, and server-side sanitization are required. File content cannot enter the system because uploads are deferred.
- A request-persistence failure shows an error and no receipt. After the CRM safely stores the request, show a success state with its reference even if confirmation email fails; retry email asynchronously up to three times and alert the operator without changing the request state.
- Every request, invitation, match, and audit event uses a stable identifier so metrics can be derived from the operational source of truth.
- Unsupported-demand metrics count only homeowners who explicitly consent to store a waitlist request. Do not create a location-demand event for people who decline storage.

### Homeowner, operator, and contractor workflow

```text
NEW
├── unsupported coverage shown -> waitlist consent -> UNSUPPORTED
└── supported coverage -> SUBMITTED
SUBMITTED
├── NEEDS_INFORMATION -> homeowner supplies missing details -> QUALIFIED
├── DECLINED -> clear reason; do not silently discard
├── CANCELLED -> consent withdrawn or homeowner cancels
├── CLOSED_ELSEWHERE -> homeowner hired someone independently
└── QUALIFIED
    ├── MATCHED -> contractor accepts -> INTRODUCED
    ├── NO_AVAILABLE_CONTRACTOR -> waitlist or manual follow-up
    └── EXPIRED -> homeowner may refresh timing and availability
```

Release one uses deterministic eligibility plus manual selection:

1. Filter contractors by active review, explicit postal/city coverage, required category, accepted project-size band, and declared availability. These are hard constraints.
2. An authenticated operator reviews the eligible set and records why a contractor was selected. No automated quality ranking is promised.
3. Send a project summary without homeowner contact details to one contractor at a time. The contractor responds through a signed, single-use invitation link bound to its contractor and invitation IDs. The token is hashed at rest, expires with the invitation, rejects replay, and becomes invalid after the first response.
4. The Ontario pilot calendar is Monday–Friday, 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. America/Toronto, excluding Ontario statutory holidays. An invitation sent outside those hours starts its four-business-hour clock at the next opening.
5. Try at most three eligible contractors. Share homeowner contact details only after acceptance and only under the recorded consent.
6. Revalidate eligibility before introduction. Ambiguous postal-code boundaries require operator confirmation. If no contractor accepts, return `NO_AVAILABLE_CONTRACTOR` and offer manual follow-up or waitlist consent.

The homeowner may correct details, cancel, withdraw sharing consent, or report hiring elsewhere through a reply link or operator-assisted process. Withdrawal stops future invitations; already-shared data is recorded and deletion requests follow the privacy policy.

### Request transition rules

| Transition | Actor | Preconditions | Notification / terminal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| New → Submitted | System | Valid fields and durable CRM write | Send receipt; recover confirmation email asynchronously |
| New → Unsupported | Homeowner and system | Valid uncovered postal code; outcome shown; explicit waitlist consent recorded | Persist waitlist request; terminal unless coverage changes |
| Submitted → Needs information | Operator | Required qualification detail missing | Ask homeowner; expires after 14 days without response |
| Submitted/Needs information → Qualified | Operator | Minimum project detail present | Begin eligibility filter and invitation sequence |
| Any pre-introduction state → Cancelled | Homeowner or operator | Cancellation or sharing-consent withdrawal | Stop future invitations; terminal |
| Any open state → Closed elsewhere | Homeowner or operator | Homeowner hired independently | Stop future invitations; terminal |
| Qualified → Matched | Operator | Contractor accepts and coverage is revalidated before committing the transition | Prepare introduction; if revalidation fails remain Qualified and continue or close No available contractor |
| Matched → Introduced | Operator | Sharing consent current and contact delivered | Record introduction; terminal for matching |
| Qualified → No available contractor | System or operator | Three eligible invitations declined/expired, or eligibility disappears | Offer waitlist/manual follow-up; terminal for current attempt |
| Open → Declined | Operator | Project outside documented product rules | Explain reason; terminal |
| Open → Expired | System | Applicable response window passes | Explain refresh path; terminal unless homeowner resubmits |

### Operations and audit requirements

- Before launch, select an authenticated CRM or equivalent operational system as the source of truth. A public spreadsheet is not acceptable.
- Roles: operator can qualify and invite; administrator can change contractor/coverage status and review audit history; contractors can respond only to their invitations.
- Every state transition requires an actor, timestamp, reason, and correlation ID. No request may disappear without a terminal or recoverable state.
- Queue views must expose new requests, expiring invitations, delivery failures, paused coverage, and overdue homeowner updates.
- Release-one service hours, queue owner, capacity limit, and escalation contact must be documented before advertising any response-time target.
- Provider selection is Phase 0 and blocks implementation estimates. Validate candidates against: stable custom IDs, role controls, consent fields, idempotent create/update, audit history, signed-link integration, timed queues, webhooks/API access, email retry visibility, export/deletion support, and event reporting.

### Privacy, retention, and communications

- Collect only fields needed to qualify and match a project. Record consent text version and timestamp separately for service processing, contractor sharing, and optional waitlist/contact.
- Show the contractor an anonymized project summary first. Reveal homeowner contact information only after acceptance and consent.
- Restrict operational access by role, use encrypted transport and managed storage, and never place homeowner data in analytics payloads or source control.
- Publish correction, cancellation, withdrawal, and deletion channels. Pilot defaults are 90-day retention for unmatched requests and 12 months after closure for matched requests, subject to legal review before production.
- Record deletions without retaining the deleted personal content. Define incident ownership and a breach-response contact before launch.
- Email is the required release-one notification channel. SMS and file attachments are deferred.
- The Ontario pilot is English-first and targets WCAG 2.2 AA for forms, status messages, keyboard use, errors, and contrast. Quebec remains inactive until the product, policies, and support experience are available in French.
- Contractor participation terms govern homeowner data after introduction: use only for the referred project, no onward sharing, reasonable security, defined retention/deletion, withdrawal cooperation, and prompt incident reporting. Contractors accept the current terms before receiving contact information.

### Operational state synchronization

- Matching queries the operational source of truth at qualification and acceptance; coverage changes must block new introductions within five minutes.
- Contractor or coverage changes emit a webhook to the site, invalidate cached eligibility, and trigger location/navigation/sitemap revalidation or rebuild.
- A city that loses eligibility must disappear from matching immediately and from crawlable navigation and sitemap output within 60 minutes.
- Failed synchronization alerts the operator and fails closed for new introductions in the affected location.

### Structured data and trust

- Replace installer-oriented `LocalBusiness` claims with organization and website markup appropriate to Tiling.ca itself.
- Do not mark Tiling.ca as a local tile contractor in cities where it only provides referrals.
- Contractor profile markup, reviews, addresses, and ratings are added only when the site stores and displays reviewed source data.
- Explain exactly how contractors are reviewed, avoid the word “verified” until a stronger policy supports it, disclose how Tiling.ca is compensated, explain how homeowner data is shared, and state what happens when no match is available.
- Use `Service` markup only for the matching/referral service or for accurately described informational service pages.

### Navigation and internal linking

- Primary navigation: Find an installer, Services, Materials, Locations, How it works, For contractors.
- Province pages list active cities first and clearly label recruiting or unavailable areas.
- City pages link to relevant canonical service and material guides, nearby active cities, the matching intake, and contractor participation information.
- Service and material pages link back only to active, indexable locations where the subject is supported.

### Sitemap and monitoring

- Generate separate logical sitemap groups for core pages, services/materials, and active location pages when volume warrants it.
- Include only canonical, indexable URLs and stable `lastModified` values based on meaningful content or coverage changes.
- Track impressions, clicks, completed intakes, qualified requests, match rate, contractor acceptance rate, time to first response, unsupported-location demand, and pages that receive traffic without successful matches.
- A location that repeatedly receives requests but cannot match them becomes a contractor-recruiting priority, not an excuse to claim coverage.

The CRM/event store is authoritative for product metrics. Search Console is authoritative for search impressions and clicks. Metrics join through stable location slugs and never through personal contact data.

### Existing URL migration

Migration occurs atomically with the equivalent new pages:

| Old route | New route | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `/areas` | `/locations` | Permanent 308 |
| `/areas/{city}` | `/locations/ontario/{city}` | Permanent 308 only when that city is Published; otherwise retain a corrected referral-language legacy page with `noindex` until activation or retirement |
| `/areas/{city}/{service}` | `/locations/ontario/{city}` | Permanent 308 only when that city is Published; otherwise retain a corrected `noindex` legacy page or redirect to the canonical non-local service guide when it fully satisfies intent |

Acceptance tests verify every existing city and city-service URL, status code, destination, final canonical, breadcrumb, and sitemap removal. Old and new canonicals must never be indexable simultaneously.

### Rollout

1. Validate five homeowner intakes and recruit three Ontario contractors using a manual concierge process.
2. Complete Phase 0: select the CRM/email destination, privacy policy, operator owner, contractor review policy, postal source, service hours, and integration proof.
3. Correct the business model across the current site and implement the Ontario registry, intake, explicit states, audit events, and failure handling.
4. Publish only existing Ontario cities that pass coverage and content gates; otherwise show them only as recruiting locations in the intake.
5. Launch the Canada hub and Ontario page, then migrate existing `/areas` URLs atomically.
6. Review the pilot metrics. Only then seed and activate another province in small batches.

Future consideration, not a release-one requirement: city-and-service pages may be evaluated after real search demand, successful matches, and local proof demonstrate distinct homeowner value.

The three-contractor assignment is a concierge-validation floor, not a publication promise. City and Ontario pages remain unindexed until the threshold-driven publication cohort satisfies the actual gates.

## Success Criteria

### Product validation

- Five real homeowners complete the tile-specific intake without guided assistance.
- At least three qualified requests are manually matched to suitable contractors.
- At least 60% of qualified requests receive an accepted contractor match during the Ontario pilot.
- Median first human response is measured during the pilot; a public SLA is added only after staffing evidence supports it.
- Every unsupported request receives an explicit outcome and optional waitlist choice.

### SEO and content quality

- Zero indexable city pages lack reviewed current coverage, unique homeowner guidance, canonical metadata, and internal links.
- Zero inactive locations appear in XML sitemaps.
- Automated tests cover every coverage-to-indexing transition.
- Automated tests cover malformed versus uncovered postal codes, idempotent submission, invitation expiry, cancellation, coverage loss, and delivery failure.
- Existing North GTA URLs retain a deliberate redirect or canonical migration path.
- Structured data validation reports no fabricated local business locations, ratings, or installer claims.

### Marketplace learning

- Record why each request matched, failed to match, or was declined.
- Record unsupported-location demand by forward sortation area without promising service.
- Review activation decisions monthly using homeowner demand and contractor availability, not keyword volume alone.

## Dependencies

- A documented contractor review policy and homeowner disclosure language.
- At least a small reviewed Ontario contractor cohort before any city is published.
- Privacy, consent, retention, deletion, incident-response, and homeowner disclosure rules reviewed before production.
- An authenticated CRM, transactional email destination, queue owner, and manual operating process for qualification, invitations, acceptance, and homeowner follow-up.
- A documented postal coverage source and explicit operator override process.
- The current local persistence remains preview-only; photo uploads are deferred.

- Decide and disclose pilot contractor pricing.
